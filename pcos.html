PCOS

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome

A common long-term hormonal condition affecting reproductive health and other aspects of health.

What is PCOS?

PCOS is described in the supplied write-up as a common long-term hormonal condition affecting the reproductive system and other aspects of health. It can affect hormone levels, menstrual cycles, ovulation, fertility, metabolism, skin and mental health.

Common Symptoms

  • Irregular or absent menstrual periods
  • Irregular or absent ovulation
  • Difficulty becoming pregnant
  • Increased facial or body hair
  • Acne or oily skin
  • Hair thinning or hair loss
  • Weight-related and metabolic changes

Other Health Risks

The write-up associates PCOS with insulin resistance, type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure, abnormal cholesterol or blood-fat levels, cardiovascular disease and mental-health concerns.

How is PCOS Diagnosed?

Diagnosis involves symptoms, medical history and appropriate clinical investigations. In adults, the write-up describes irregular or absent ovulation, evidence of increased androgen activity, and polycystic ovarian morphology as features that may be considered after other causes have been excluded.

Treatment and Management

There is no single treatment for everyone. Management depends on symptoms, health needs and reproductive goals and may address menstrual cycles, androgen-related symptoms, metabolic health, fertility, mental-health concerns and longer-term risks.

Important: Professional medical care is important because PCOS can affect several aspects of health.
